1. Overview & Game Modes

HexChess is a deterministic, perfect-information abstract strategy game played on a 37-hex hexagonal board. This PRD specifies a self-contained, playable HTML/CSS/JavaScript web application designed for human play, AI experimentation, and structured game-record generation for replay or machine learning training. Blue always moves first, and moves are final (no Undo).

2-Player Hot Seat

Two human players alternate turns on the same device. Ambient board glow and the bottom status pill clearly indicate whose turn it is.

Player vs. AI

Human is randomly assigned Blue or Orange at game start (no color selector). Configurable AI thinking delay in Settings keeps moves easy to follow.

AI vs. AI

Pits the selected AI tier against itself using the Speed setting in Settings—ideal for watching matches or generating ML training records.

2. Board Geometry & Cubic Coordinates

Matching HexChessBoard.png, the board consists of 37 flat-top hexagons forming 4–5–6–7–6–5–4 vertical columns (flat sides on the left/right edges of the board, points at the top and bottom). Internally, every hex is addressed using Cubic Coordinates (q, r, s) satisfying q + r + s = 0 and max(|q|, |r|, |s|) ≤ 3.

  • Canonical Index Order (0..36): Column-by-column left-to-right (q = -3..+3), top-to-bottom within each column (r ascending). Under this ordering, the Center Hex (0,0,0) is index 18, the top Neutral Pusher (0,-3,3) is index 15, and the bottom Neutral Pusher (0,3,-3) is index 21.
  • Zones by Distance d = max(|q|, |r|, |s|): d=0 Center, d=1 Safe Zone Ring (6 hexes), d=2 Inner Ring (12 hexes), d=3 Outer Rim (18 hexes).
  • 6 Unit Direction Vectors (Flat-Top): Up (0,-1,+1), Down (0,+1,-1), Up-Right (+1,-1,0), Down-Right (+1,0,-1), Up-Left (-1,0,+1), Down-Left (-1,+1,0).

3. Core Rules & Edge Cases

Each player begins with 4 Regular Tiles and 2 Pusher Tiles on their bench, while 2 Neutral Pusher Tiles start on the board at the top (0,-3,3) and bottom (0,3,-3) corners. On each turn, a player performs an optional Pusher Action (place on any empty non-center hex, or move a friendly/neutral Pusher 1 hex), followed by a mandatory Regular Action (place on an empty Outer Rim hex, or move a Regular/Promoted Tile).

  • Implicit Pusher Skip: At the start of a turn, clicking any Regular Tile (bench or board) directly executes the Regular Action and skips Step 1—no "Skip" button is required, and skipped Pusher steps are omitted from the JSON history.
  • Pusher Collision Physics: Moving a Pusher directly into an adjacent Pusher is illegal. In a chain [Pusher] → [A] → [B] → [Other Pusher], tile B is crushed (captured), tile A advances into B's vacated hex, and the pushing Pusher advances into A's hex while the stationary Pusher does not move.
  • Immediate Victory & Self-Elimination: Victory is checked immediately after every action (including Step 1 Pusher moves). Eliminating your opponent's last Regular Tile wins immediately; eliminating your own last Regular Tile is legal and causes an immediate loss.
  • Draw Conditions: The game ends in a draw on Stalemate (no legal Regular Action on your turn) OR after 30 consecutive full turns (30 Blue + 30 Orange turns) with no placement or capture.

Chain Push & Edge Capture

A Pusher moves 1 hex into an occupied hex, shifting the entire contiguous line of Regular/Promoted tiles 1 hex. Any tile pushed off the board edge is captured.

Pusher Collision (Crush)

Pushers cannot be pushed. If a pushed tile would collide with another Pusher, that tile is crushed (captured) and the chain stops—even on a Safe Zone.

Center Push-Promotion

Pushers can never enter the Center Hex, but they can push a Regular Tile into it, immediately promoting it (shown with a gold/white double border).

4. UI Architecture, Modals & Animations

Main Game Screen

  • Initial Load: Opens directly with the New Game modal overlaid on the board.
  • Ambient Turn Glow: Subtle pulsing blue radial gradient under the board on Blue's turn; pulsing orange radial gradient on Orange's turn.
  • Left Vertical Sidebar Pill: New Game (+), Resign (red flag), Rules (blue scroll), Settings (gear), vertical HEXCHESS gradient title, and short Game ID (e.g., #3HKK).
  • Player Benches (Left & Right): 4 Regular Tile slots + divider + 2 Pusher Tile slots. Regular slots show 3 distinct states:
    • Available on bench: Full bright colored hex.
    • Deployed on board: Empty dark outlined hex.
    • Captured: Smaller dim colored hex with a subtle red ×.
  • Bottom Status Pill: Displays active player and current sub-phase (e.g., Blue's Turn, plus an indicator when Step 1 Pusher has already been used).
  • Action-First Disambiguation: Clicking an adjacent friendly piece while a Pusher is selected executes the push. Deselect by clicking the Pusher again, clicking a non-target hex, or right-clicking.
  • No Undo: Moves are final once executed; no Undo button is provided or allowed.

Smooth Piece Animations

All board state transitions must be smoothly animated rather than snapping instantaneously:

  • Sliding Movement: Regular, Promoted, and Pusher tiles glide smoothly along the pixel vector from their origin hex to their destination hex (e.g., ~220–280ms ease-out).
  • Bench Placement: Pieces placed from a player's bench animate smoothly into the target hex with a subtle scale-in/pop effect.
  • Synchronized Chain Push: When a Pusher pushes a line of tiles, the Pusher and every pushed tile slide in unison by 1 hex in the push direction.
  • Edge Fall ("Spiral into Darkness"): Any piece pushed off the edge of the board slides past the rim and then spirals downward into darkness (combined rotation + scale-down to 0 + fade-out over ~450ms) before being marked captured on its bench.
  • Crush & Capture: Captured or crushed pieces play a brief impact/fade-out animation before disappearing; a Regular Tile promoted on the Center Hex animates its gold/white inner ring fading/pulsing in.

5. AI Architecture & Lookahead Feasibility

Every AI agent implements a single stateless, ML-ready interface that consumes only the 42-element compact board state (which already encodes turnPhase) and outputs one atomic action: 

chooseAction(compactState, agentType) → Action  // SKIP_PUSHER | PLACE_PUSHER | MOVE_PUSHER | PLACE_REGULAR | MOVE_REGULAR

Agent Tiers

  • Random Bot: Selects uniformly from all legal actions in the current phase. Ideal for sanity testing and baseline dataset generation.
  • Heuristic + Alpha-Beta Bot (2-Turn / 4-Ply Lookahead): Evaluates positions using:
    1. Material & Eliminations: Highest priority to pushing enemies off the board, crushing them against Pushers, or capturing with Regular/Promoted tiles.
    2. Danger Avoidance: Penalizes deploying or moving onto hexes vulnerable to immediate capture or 1-step push-off/crush (automatically reinforced by 4-ply lookahead).
    3. Promotion & Safe Zones: Rewards promoting on the Center Hex and occupying Safe Zone hexes.
  • Future ML / Neural Agent: Drop-in replacement using the exact same 42-element input vector and atomic action space, trained on saved records/*.json games.

Branching Factor & Lookahead

Splitting each turn into 2 atomic plies (Pusher → Regular) keeps the branching factor at b ≈ 20–30 per ply (~400–800 per full turn). With Alpha-Beta pruning + move ordering (captures/crushes first), effective branching drops to beff ≈ 5–6:

6. Minimal State Encoding & Game Records

Every move in the game record includes both a human-readable action description and a minimal canonical state encoding that contains 100% of the information needed to render the board, both benches (including captured slots), and the active turn/phase—or to feed directly into an ML model.

Minimal 42-Element State Encoding (123 Bits / 16 Bytes Packed)

Each of the 37 hexes (in fixed canonical cubic order, index 0..36) has exactly 8 possible states (3 bits):

Code01234567
Hex StateEmptyBlue RegBlue PromoBlue PushOrange RegOrange PromoOrange PushNeutral Push

Appending just 5 small integers completes the entire game state:

  • Bench Counts (4 integers): [bReg (0..4), bPush (0..2), oReg (0..4), oPush (0..2)]. Captured counts are derived automatically (capturedBlue = 4 - bReg - blueOnBoard), so no extra bits are needed!
  • Turn & Phase (1 integer, 2 bits): 0 = Blue (Pusher Optional), 1 = Blue (Regular Only), 2 = Orange (Pusher Optional), 3 = Orange (Regular Only).
  • Total Size: 42 integers (37 + 4 + 1), serializable as a compact 42-element array [7,0,0,...,7, 4,2,4,2, 0] or a 44-char string "700...007|4242|0". Bit-packed, the entire game state fits in 123 bits (16 bytes).

Saving to the records/ Folder

  • Primary (File System Access API): In Chrome/Edge, the user picks the local records/ folder once (in Settings or Game Over). After that, clicking Save Record (or enabling Auto-Save) writes hexchess_YYYYMMDD_HHMMSS_<id>.json directly into records/.
  • Fallback (All Browsers): Downloads the JSON file directly with the same structured filename.

7. Modular File Structure

The playable web application will use a clean, zero-build modular structure:

HexChess/
├── index.html      # Main page shell, SVG board container, benches, sidebar & modals
├── styles.css      # Dark navy theme, pulsing Blue/Orange turn glow, piece & spiral animations
├── game.js         # Pure HexChess rules engine, cubic math, 42-element state codec, DOM renderer & record saver
├── ai.js           # Stateless AI agents (Random, Heuristic/Minimax) implementing chooseTurn(state)
└── records/        # Saved JSON game records for replay & ML training
